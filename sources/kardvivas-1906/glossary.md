# Glossary — *Kardvívás* (Leszák Károly, 1906)

Hungarian terms of the book and the English used in the translation, for the part translated so far (Foreword to §16). The translation is the authority; this page is an index and quick reference. Where a table restates the text, the § reference says where to check it.

**How the English words were chosen**

- *Kard* is "sabre" throughout. The Hungarian word covers "sword" in general, but the book is about the fencing sabre (*vívókard*, with a basket guard).
- The nine named sabre positions use the usual English fencing spellings: tierce, seconde, prime, quarte, quinte, sixte, septime (Hungarian *terc, szekond, prím, kvárt, kvint, szext, szeptim*).
- *Állás* is "stance" for the whole-body stances (*alapállás, vívóállás, pihenő állás*) and "position" for sabre positions (*terc-állás*, "tierce position") and in the command *Állás!* ("Position!").
- *Kardhelyzet* (numbered 1–6) and *kardállás* (named: tierce, seconde…) are two different things in the book, so they get two words: **sabre attitude** and **sabre position**.
- A command has a preparatory part and an executive word. The executive word is in italics, as in the print: "Tierce – *one!*", "Tierce *position!*".

## The sabre positions (*kardállások*)

§11 describes the nine positions (the book numbers I–VIII; septime has no number); §12 says which openings each protects. The figures are photographs; §11 says they show the positions as practised in the fencing stance ("the accompanying drawings were made accordingly"). The book does not say who is pictured. Fig. 15 is the only photograph in a white jacket.

| Position | Hungarian | Book no. | Fig. | Hand and sabre (restated from §11) | Protects (§12) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tierce** | terc | I | 7 (also 17) | Basket drawn back by moderately bending the elbow, so that hand and forearm are covered by the basket (elbow pressed down); sabre in the 6th attitude; point at the height of the right eye, in the line | outer upper openings |
| **Seconde** | szekond | II | 8 | Sabre brought back in a circle in front of face and chest, then swung forward and to the right: point level with the right hip, in the low line; blade "obliquely to the right and up" (the text has no verb, so it is unclear whether this is the slope of the blade or the direction of the edge; see the §11 note); upper edge of the basket level with the right shoulder; arm moderately bent | outer lower openings |
| **Prime** | prím | III | 9 | Swung forward and to the left: point in the low line, level with the left knee; edge faces obliquely left and up; upper edge of the basket at left-shoulder height; arm moderately bent | inner lower openings |
| **Quinte** | kvint | IV | 10 | Basket in front of the right temple; point a little left of, and higher than, the left temple; arm moderately bent | the head |
| **Quarte** | kvárt | V | 11 | Basket drawn sharply in front of the pit of the stomach; blade in the 5th attitude; elbow sinks to the hip; forearm horizontal, half-left; point to the left, outside the line, level with the left eye | inner upper openings |
| **Low tierce** | mélyterc | VI | 12 | Elbow rests on the right hip; forearm slightly down; lower edge of the basket lower than the right hip; edge turned outward; point level with the right temple, outside the line | outer lower openings |
| **Low quarte (1)** | mélykvárt 1. | VII.1 | 13 | Elbow pressed to the hip; forearm to the left (back) and down; edge left and down; point outside the line at eye height | the belly |
| **Low quarte (2)** | mélykvárt 2. | VII.2 | 14 | Bent arm jerked down so that the strong of the blade, horizontal with the point fully left and the edge down, is below the hip ("the reverse of the quinte") | against a low thrust |
| **Sixte** | szext | VIII | 15 | From tierce: elbow raised toward the left shoulder, forearm vertical, hand toward the left eye; point rises back in an arc and sinks to the right almost to horizontal; edge faces up | the head |
| **Septime** | szeptim | — | 16 | Begun as sixte, then the blade taken almost vertically downward, hand to the right of the right temple at head height ("yielding parry", *engedő hárítás*) | outer upper openings |

Low tierce, low quarte (1, 2), sixte and septime are "auxiliary parries" (*kisegítő hárítás*) used only in school fencing and free fencing (§11).

## The sabre attitudes (*kardhelyzetek*)

The roll of the arm and blade about the arm's axis (§6). Edge direction as seen by the fencer, as in the text.

| Attitude | Edge of the blade faces |
|---|---|
| 1st | up (point goes slightly down) |
| 2nd | right (= the basic stance) |
| 3rd | down (point slightly up) |
| 4th | fully left (blade horizontal, point slightly right) |
| 5th | between 3rd and 4th |
| 6th | between 2nd and 3rd |

Tierce is in the 6th attitude and quarte in the 5th (§11).

### Openings (*rések*)

The parts of the opponent's body above the hip that may be attacked (§9).

| Hungarian | English | Parts |
|---|---|---|
| külső felső rések | outer upper openings | head, outer face, outer upper arm and hand |
| külső alsó rések | outer lower openings | side, lower arm and hand underneath |
| belső felső rések | inner upper openings | head, inner face, chest, arm and hand on the inside |
| belső alsó rések | inner lower openings | chest and belly |

In the first and second lists, "arm" translates *kar* qualified by a region word; it is not necessarily the anatomical upper arm or forearm (see the note in §9).
